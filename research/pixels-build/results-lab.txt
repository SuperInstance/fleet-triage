E8  THE CHURN CLAIM, three ways
    the seed asserts that `loss_b < loss_a` "prevents macro weights from
    alternating rapidly when noise boundaries are tight".

    the known-good fix offered 20x        promotions = 1   (converges)
    two candidates alternating 20x       promotions = 1   (no flip-flop)
    winning margin over 600 proposals (loss is 100 per wrong bit):
      <=100 (1 bit)    2   <=200    0   <=400    7   <=800    4   <=1600    8   >1600   73
      accepted 94 of 600; 2 of those rest on a SINGLE bit.
    -> the guard is monotone and it does stop oscillation: both claims hold.
       What it does not have is a floor.  A proposal that wins by one bit
       out of 512 is promoted, and `loss_b < loss_a` cannot tell that from a
       proposal that wins by a thousand.  A margin is the only thing that
       makes the difference visible, and the seed does not have one.
